NutriMama AI Educational Reference — Food Safety During Pregnancy
(Original educational summary prepared for NutriMama AI, reflecting broadly
published food-safety consensus for pregnancy. Not a verbatim reproduction of any
single official guideline. Educational use only.)

Why Food Safety Gets Extra Attention in Pregnancy
Pregnancy involves changes to the immune system that can make certain foodborne
illnesses (like listeriosis, salmonellosis, and toxoplasmosis) more likely to
cause complications for both the pregnant person and the fetus, even when the
illness would be mild in a non-pregnant adult. This is why food safety guidance
during pregnancy is often more conservative than general food safety advice.

Foods Commonly Advised to Avoid
- Unpasteurized dairy products and soft cheeses made from unpasteurized milk
  (certain brie, camembert, blue-veined cheeses, queso fresco) due to Listeria risk.
  Versions clearly labeled as made from pasteurized milk are generally considered
  acceptable.
- Raw or undercooked eggs, due to Salmonella risk. Cook eggs until both the yolk
  and white are firm; be cautious with homemade foods containing raw egg
  (some raw cookie dough, some homemade mayonnaise or Caesar dressing).
- Raw or undercooked meat, poultry, and seafood (including sushi/sashimi with raw
  fish), due to bacterial and parasite risk.
- Deli meats and hot dogs, unless heated until steaming hot, due to Listeria risk.
- Unwashed produce, due to potential Toxoplasma or bacterial contamination —
  thorough washing is commonly advised.
- Alcohol — no amount has been established as safe during pregnancy.
- Unpasteurized juices and ciders.

High-Mercury Fish
Certain fish accumulate higher mercury levels and are commonly advised against
during pregnancy, including shark, swordfish, king mackerel, and tilefish.
Low-mercury options like salmon, shrimp, tilapia, and canned light tuna are
generally considered fine in moderation (roughly 2-3 servings per week),
providing beneficial omega-3s.

Caffeine
Most guidance suggests limiting caffeine to around 200 mg per day during
pregnancy — roughly one 12-ounce cup of coffee — based on associations between
higher caffeine intake and increased risk of miscarriage or low birth weight
observed in some studies.

Safe Food Handling Practices
- Wash hands, utensils, and surfaces thoroughly before and after handling raw
  food.
- Keep raw meat separate from other foods (separate cutting boards where
  possible).
- Cook meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs to safe internal temperatures.
- Refrigerate leftovers promptly and reheat thoroughly before eating.
- Avoid unwashed or unpeeled produce when eating away from home if washing
  practices are uncertain.

A Note on Herbal Products
Safety data on herbal teas and supplements during pregnancy is often limited or
inconsistent between herbs — ginger and peppermint tea in moderation are commonly
considered acceptable by many providers, but many other herbal products lack
sufficient safety data and are best discussed with a healthcare provider before
regular use.

When Uncertain
If a specific food isn't covered by clear guidance, a generally conservative
approach — checking with a healthcare provider, or choosing a well-established
safe alternative — is reasonable during pregnancy.
